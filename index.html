01 — THE SCALE

This isn't a toy dataset. It's a national scheme with real money and no room to hand-check it.

Each of India's 543 Lok Sabha and roughly 245 Rajya Sabha members is entitled to ₹5 crore a year, paid straight to district authorities in two installments, non-lapsable so unspent balances just carry forward and pile up. That's before you count interest income, carried-forward balances, and disaster-relief top-ups. Multiply that by lakhs of individual works — roads, handpumps, school rooms, crematorium walls — each with its own sanction, cost estimate, contractor, and completion certificate, and you get a dataset no team of auditors can read line by line.

02 — THE EVIDENCE

This isn't a hypothetical fraud risk. It's a documented, repeating one.

The Comptroller and Auditor General has audited MPLADS three times and flagged the same category of failures each time — the 2010–11 report even calls out that the "systemic weaknesses... had persisted since inception 17 years ago." That repetition is itself the strongest argument for automation: manual, periodic audits keep re-discovering the same problem years apart, on a scheme that pays out every single day in between.

1998
First CAG performance audit of MPLADS. Weak monitoring and sanctioning gaps flagged for the first time.
2001
Second audit. Same category of lapses recur — action-taken notes on the 1998 report were still pending.
2010–11
Third audit explicitly states the weaknesses have "persisted since inception," 17 years on. Action-taken note on the 2001 report took 8 years to even arrive.

Today

Ghost & duplicate works

Gujarat, 2018

CAG Report No. 4 found ₹5.93 crore paid to a cooperative society without any tender, plus ₹84.53 lakh billed for works in a village adopted under the scheme. One line item claimed renovation of the same panchayat building twice a year apart. Auditors visited several sanctioned sites and found no construction had happened at all.

SOURCE: CAG Report No. 4 of 2018 (Gujarat)
Sanction bypass

Jammu & Kashmir

CAG's district-level audit found 558 works executed without any MP recommendation at all — the single largest share among the states audited. District authorities' own explanation: "urgency of work," approved on a local legislator's assurance, MP's sign-off backfilled later.

SOURCE: CAG MPLADS performance audit, district sampling
Utilization anomaly

Delhi, 16th Lok Sabha

Of ₹187 crore entitled to Delhi's seven MPs, ₹133 crore sat unspent. Within the same city, same tenure, same rules: one MP had drawn down 98% of his entitlement, another under 43% — a gap flagged only because a journalist pulled the numbers, not because a system did.

03 — WHY THIS IS AN ML PROBLEM, NOT JUST A DASHBOARD

Every failure mode above is a pattern in structured data. That's exactly what anomaly detection is for.

None of these cases needed a whistleblower with inside access — they needed someone to notice a pattern across thousands of rows. That's a task suited to models, not headcount.

01

Cost-estimate outlier detection

Flag works whose sanctioned cost sits far outside the distribution for comparable work type, material, and district — a wall that costs 4x the district median wall.

→ maps to duplicate-billing pattern (Gujarat case)
02

Vendor / implementing-agency graph analysis

Cluster implementing agencies and contractors by shared bank details, addresses, or office-bearers to surface shell entities winning work repeatedly without competition.

→ maps to no-tender fraud pattern (Gujarat case)
03

Process-sequence deviation

Every legitimate work has an expected sequence: recommendation → sanction → fund release → execution → completion. Flag works where a step is missing or out of order.

→ maps to unsanctioned-works pattern (J&K case)
04

Utilization-rate anomaly & delay prediction

Peer-compare MPs and districts on spend velocity and completion time; predict which in-progress works are trending toward multi-year stalling before it happens.

→ maps to utilization-gap pattern (Delhi case)
04 — SOLUTION APPROACH, TRACED THROUGH A REAL CASE

Not a hypothetical. Here's how the pipeline would have caught this one.

Every stage below maps to a real signal in a real CAG-documented case — not a made-up demo scenario.

CAG Report No. 4 of 2018 — Gujarat

An implementing agency, Sharda Majoor Kamdar Mandli, claimed ₹45.20 lakh to renovate a panchayat building in Anand district in FY 2015–16 — then claimed the same renovation again the following year. Across nine villages it received ₹5.93 crore without a single competitive tender, and auditors later found some sanctioned sites had no construction at all. CAG only surfaced this in a 2018 audit — years after both payments had gone out.

1
Ingest

The second sanction lands in the MIS

Same building, same village, same implementing agency, same ₹45.20L amount — one financial year apart. On its own, a human reviewer scanning thousands of rows a year later has no reason to connect the two.

work_title: "Renovation, Gram Panchayat Bldg, Anand"
agency: "Sharda Majoor Kamdar Mandli" · fy: 2016-17
2
Duplicate detector

Title + location + agency similarity check

An NLP similarity model compares the new record against prior sanctions for the same agency and location. It scores this pair at ~0.97 similarity — near-identical.

similarity_score: 0.97 → FLAG: possible re-billing
3
Procurement rule check

Was this agency ever competitively selected?

A rule engine looks for a tender record behind each sanction. There isn't one — for either payment — and this agency has now drawn ₹5.93 crore cumulative without ever appearing in a tender record.

tender_record: none → FLAG: IA selection bypass
4
Vendor graph

One agency, nine villages, zero competitors

Graphing implementing agencies against the works they're assigned shows this agency tied to 9 villages under one MP's recommendations, with no competing bidder ever recorded across any of them.

agency_works: 9 · competing_bids: 0 → FLAG: vendor concentration
5
Risk scoring

Three weak signals become one strong one

Duplicate-billing score, no-tender flag, and vendor concentration combine into a composite risk score. The Gemini API turns the rule outputs into a plain-language note a district clerk can actually read.

risk_score: 92/100 — HIGH RISK
"Repeat billing for a previously completed
renovation, sanctioned without competitive tender."
6
Alert

The flag appears before the money moves

A red flag appears on the District Authority's dashboard the moment the second sanction is entered — before payment is released. The Ministry's dashboard rolls it into one cluster: 1 agency, 9 villages, ₹5.93 crore.

What actually happened
Years later

CAG's performance audit surfaced this case in its 2018 report — after both payments had already been disbursed and the funds were gone.

What the pipeline does
Before disbursal

The duplicate check runs the moment the second sanction is entered — flagging it while the payment can still be stopped, not years after it can't.

08 — WHY WE SHOULD PICK 26102

Compared to the other options on the table, this one is unusually low-risk to build and hard to argue against in judging.

✓

The dataset already exists and is public

mplads.mospi.gov.in publishes live sanction, expenditure, and work-status data. No synthetic data, no scraping fight, no "we couldn't get real data" excuse in the demo.

✓

A real ministry wrote this problem statement

MoSPI's Data Informatics & Innovation Division is the sponsoring department — there's a plausible path from prototype to something actually looked at, which is exactly what SIH-style judges reward.

✓

The scope splits cleanly across a team

Data pipeline, ML/anomaly models, risk-scoring backend, and multi-role dashboards are four genuinely separable workstreams — everyone gets a real piece, not busywork.

✓

The story tells itself

We're not pitching "AI for X" in the abstract — we can open with a real CAG-documented fraud case and show, concretely, which model would have caught it.

✓

It's less contested than the obvious picks

Chatbots, generic agri-tech, and health-symptom checkers are the themes every other team defaults to. A financial-integrity / GovTech anomaly system stands out in a room full of those.

✓

The tech is achievable in hackathon time

React + Node.js + MongoDB is a stack most of us already know; scikit-learn handles the anomaly models without training from scratch, and the Gemini API can generate the plain-language "why this was flagged" explanations for free.
